<Section id="overview">

A non-modal panel anchored to a trigger. It holds content that needs more room than a menu, such as a small form, a filter
set or an explanation, and it never blocks the page: no focus trap, no scroll lock, nothing made inert.

<Canvas of={Stories.Open} />

### Usage

`trigger` is a function that receives the props the trigger needs (`ref`, `onClick`, `aria-expanded`, `aria-controls`,
`aria-haspopup`) and returns the element, so any button component works and none is imposed.

```tsx
import { Popover } from "@lufia/design-system/components/Popover";

<Popover label="Filters" trigger={(props) => <Button {...props}>Filters</Button>}>
  <FilterForm />
</Popover>;
```

Strings in the snippet are plain literals. In your app they arrive through props from your copy layer. The `ref` has to
reach the trigger's DOM element: it is what the panel is placed against and where focus returns.

</Section>

### Placement

`placement` is the side of the trigger (`top`, `bottom`, `start`, `end`; the last two follow the writing direction) and
`align` lines the panel up along the other axis (`start`, `center`, `end`).

<Canvas of={Stories.PlacedAbove} />
<Canvas of={Stories.PlacedAtEnd} />
<Canvas of={Stories.AlignedToEnd} />

The panel stays in the viewport. It flips to the opposite side when it does not fit on the requested one and the opposite
has more room, shifts along the other axis to stay inside the viewport margin, and scrolls inside when even the chosen side
is shorter than its content. It follows the trigger on scroll (of any ancestor) and resize.

### Portal and clipping

The open panel is portalled to `document.body`, or into the open modal the trigger sits in (so the modal's focus trap and
inert page do not lock it out), and placed with `position: fixed`. An ancestor with `overflow: hidden` cannot clip it. Find
it by role (`getByRole("dialog", { name })`), not inside the trigger's parent. What the trigger's subtree sets that the body
does not is copied onto the panel: a nested `data-lufia-theme` or `data-lufia-mode` scope, `dir` and `lang`, and any
`--lufia-*` custom properties set inline. Until the component has mounted (server render, hydration) the panel is not
rendered. It stacks at `--lufia-z-dropdown`.

<Canvas of={Stories.InClippingCard} />

### Controlled

Pass `open` and `onOpenChange` to keep the state in the caller, for example to close it after an action or to open it from
elsewhere. `defaultOpen` starts it open without moving focus.

<Canvas of={Stories.ControlledByTheCaller} />

### Not a sheet on phones

`Popover` has no sheet presentation. `Modal` shows a bottom sheet on phones, but that is a modal surface with a focus trap
and an inert page, which a non-modal panel must not have, and it is not reusable for this. On a narrow screen the panel keeps
its anchor and is limited to the viewport width. For a list of actions that becomes a sheet, use `MoreActions`.

### Keyboard

| Key           | Where                      | Action                                                                              |
| ------------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Enter / Space | Trigger                    | Opens or closes the panel; focus moves to the first control inside (or the panel)   |
| Escape        | Anywhere                   | Closes the panel and returns focus to the trigger                                   |
| Tab           | Last control in the panel  | Focus goes to the trigger and on to the next element after it; the panel stays open |
| Shift+Tab     | First control in the panel | Focus goes back to the trigger; the panel stays open                                |

A press outside closes the panel and leaves focus where the press put it. Escape and outside presses go through `useDismiss`
and the shared layer stack, so inside a `Modal` Escape closes the popover first and the modal on the next press.

### Accessibility

- The trigger carries `aria-expanded` and `aria-haspopup="dialog"`, and `aria-controls` while the panel is rendered.
- The panel is `role="dialog"` named by `label`. It is not `aria-modal`: the page behind stays in the accessibility tree
  and operable. Focus moves in on open (so a screen reader announces the dialog) and returns to the trigger on Escape, and when
  the panel is closed from inside while focus was in it.
- The panel is portalled to the end of the body, so the browser's own Tab order would skip the rest of the page; leaving the
  panel at either end goes through the trigger instead.
- The panel is `inert` while it animates out. Under reduced motion it appears and disappears without the scale and fade. In
  forced-colors mode its border is `CanvasText`.
- The panel is not closed when the trigger scrolls out of view; it keeps following it and stays clamped inside the viewport.
  Close it from `onOpenChange` or your own scroll handling when that matters.
- This is the non-modal variant of the WAI-ARIA dialog pattern; the APG documents only the modal one, so the focus rules above
  are the ones this component follows.

<Section id="guidelines">

### When to use

- Contextual content tied to one control: a few filters, a short form, a rich explanation.
- Something the person may want to consult while still working on the page.

### When not to use

- A list of actions: use `MoreActions`.
- A question that must be answered, or content that needs the whole page's attention: use `Modal` or `ConfirmDialog`.
- A one-line hint: use a tooltip or help text.
- Long flows or large forms: use a page or a `Modal`.

### Content guidelines

Name the panel by what it holds ("Filters", "Share settings"). Keep it short; put the primary action last.
